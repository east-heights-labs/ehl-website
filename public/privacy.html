§2 — Information We Collect

We collect the minimum information necessary to operate the App. The categories below describe what we collect, when, and why.

§2.1 Device UUID (Anonymous Identifier)

When you first launch the App, we generate and assign a random, device-specific UUID (“Device UUID”) to your installation. This UUID:

  • Is randomly generated on first launch and stored on your device;
  • Is not your device’s hardware identifier (IDFA, IDFV, or serial number);
  • Is used to associate your in-app activity (favorites, “I’m Going” confirmations, stage time reports) with your device without requiring an account;
  • Is transmitted to our servers to record your activity and retrieve your preferences;
  • Is not shared with third parties for advertising or profiling purposes.

We may retain multiple UUIDs associated with a single account if you use the App on multiple devices or reinstall the App (see §2.4 and §6).

§2.2 Sign In with Apple (Optional)

We offer Sign In with Apple as an optional authentication method. You are not required to sign in to use the core features of the App. If you choose to sign in:

2.2.1 What Apple Provides to Us

Apple’s Sign In with Apple service provides us with:

  • Stable Account Identifier (sub claim): A stable, anonymized identifier unique to your Apple ID for our App. This is not your Apple ID itself and cannot be used to identify you outside of our App.
  • Email address (optional): Apple may provide a real or Apple-generated relay email address, only if you choose to share it. You may opt to hide your real email using Apple’s Private Relay feature.
  • Display name (optional): A name you choose to share with us. You are not required to share your real name.

2.2.2 What We Do Not Receive

We do not receive:

  • Your Apple ID or iCloud account credentials;
  • Your Apple ID password or any authentication credentials;
  • Payment method information from Apple;
  • Any other Apple account data beyond what is explicitly listed in §2.2.1.

2.2.3 How We Use Sign In with Apple Data

We use your Apple sub claim, email (if provided), and display name (if provided) solely to:

  • Authenticate you within the App;
  • Provide account-linked features (synchronized favorites, subscription entitlements, activity history);
  • Contact you if you have provided an email and have opted in to communications; and
  • Comply with our legal obligations.

We do not share your Apple sub claim, email, or display name with third parties except as described in §4.

§2.3 Location Data

The App requests access to your device’s location to show live music events near you.

2.3.1 How Location Is Used

  • We use Apple’s CoreLocation framework to access your device’s current location, subject to the iOS permission you grant.
  • We use Apple Maps (MapKit) and MKLocalSearch for map display and location-based searches. These features may transmit your location or search queries to Apple’s servers, governed by Apple’s Privacy Policy (see §5.1).
  • Location is used to calculate event proximity and display a map of nearby events.

2.3.2 What We Do Not Do With Location

  • We do not store your precise location on our servers.
  • We do not log a history of your location.
  • We do not sell, share, or transmit your location to advertisers or data brokers.
  • Location data is used transiently for proximity queries and is not retained after the query is resolved.

2.3.3 Permission Control

You may grant “While Using” or “Always” location access, or deny location access entirely through iOS Settings. The App functions with reduced capability (no proximity sorting, no “near me” features) if location is denied.

§2.4 Account Activity and UUID History

When you sign in with Apple, we link your anonymous activity history to your account. Specifically:

  • All Device UUIDs previously used on your device(s) are associated with your account, along with first-seen and last-seen timestamps for each UUID;
  • All in-app activity previously attributed to those UUIDs (favorites, “I’m Going” confirmations, stage time reports) is backfilled to your account; and
  • If you use the App on additional devices after signing in, new Device UUIDs generated on those devices are also associated with your account.

This linkage exists to provide a seamless cross-device experience. We retain your UUID history for the duration of your account and delete it upon account deletion (see §6.2).

§2.5 Community Contributions

If you submit any of the following, we collect and store that content:

  • Stage time reports: Reported or corrected set times for artists at an event;
  • Venue corrections: Suggested corrections to venue information; and
  • “I’m Going” confirmations: An indication that you plan to attend an event.

Community contributions are associated with your Device UUID (anonymous users) or your account (signed-in users). You retain ownership of your contributions, but grant East Heights Labs a worldwide, royalty-free, perpetual, irrevocable license to use, display, and distribute your contributions as described in our Terms of Service (§4.2 of the ToS).

We may retain anonymized, de-identified community contributions (stage time reports, venue corrections) even after account deletion, as provided in §6.4.

§2.6 Subscription and Entitlement Data

If you subscribe to ShowHunter, we collect and store:

  • Your current subscription tier and its source (e.g., App Store, promo code);
  • Entitlement grant history (when each tier was granted, modified, or revoked);
  • Subscription expiry dates; and
  • Promo code redemption history (code used, redemption date).

Subscription and billing processing is handled by Apple’s StoreKit / App Store (see §5.5). We do not receive your payment card information. We receive only transaction identifiers and entitlement status from Apple.

Entitlement data is linked to your account and used solely to provision subscription benefits. It is deleted upon account deletion (see §6.2).

§2.7 Usage Data and Diagnostics

Currently: We do not collect analytics or behavioral telemetry in the alpha version of the App.

In the future: If we add analytics, we will update this Policy before doing so and, where required by law, obtain your consent. Any analytics we implement will be described in this section with specificity (vendor, data collected, retention period, opt-out mechanism).

Apple may collect app crash reports and usage data through its own frameworks (e.g., MetricKit, TestFlight diagnostics). This data is governed by Apple’s Privacy Policy and your iOS privacy settings.

§2.8 Communications Data

If you contact us by email (e.g., to request account deletion or report an issue), we collect and retain the content of that communication, including your email address, for the purpose of responding and maintaining a record of the interaction. We do not use this information for marketing without your consent.

Appendix A — Open Items for Attorney Review

The following items require attorney judgment and should be resolved before this Policy is published. Items are numbered for reference in counsel’s review.

Item 1 — TDPSA Controller/Processor Threshold Analysis

The Texas Data Privacy and Security Act applies to controllers that conduct business in Texas and either: (a) process personal data of 100,000+ consumers per year; or (b) process personal data of 25,000+ consumers per year and derive over 25% of gross revenue from the sale of personal data. Because we do not sell personal data, prong (b) likely does not apply. Prong (a) depends on user volume. Counsel should confirm: (i) whether the app is currently subject to the TDPSA at alpha stage; (ii) whether proactive compliance (included in this draft) is the appropriate posture; and (iii) whether any exemptions apply.

Item 2 — CCPA Applicability Threshold

The CCPA/CPRA applies to businesses that: (a) have annual gross revenues over $25 million; (b) buy, sell, receive, or share personal information of 100,000+ consumers or households annually; or (c) derive 50%+ of annual revenues from selling consumers’ personal information. East Heights Labs is pre-revenue. Counsel should confirm: (i) whether CCPA disclosures are appropriate at this stage as a forward-looking measure; (ii) whether any safe harbor or exemption applies to small businesses during the pre-revenue period; and (iii) whether proactive inclusion creates unintended legal obligations.

Item 3 — Inactive Account Retention Policy

We have not yet defined a retention period for accounts with no activity. Counsel should advise on: (i) best practices for inactive account retention (e.g., 2-year inactivity trigger); (ii) whether TDPSA or other applicable law requires a defined retention limit; and (iii) whether a user notice and grace period should precede deletion.

Item 4 — Apple Sign In with Apple Compliance Review

Apple’s App Store Review Guidelines (Guideline 4.8) and Sign In with Apple requirements impose specific obligations on apps that use Sign In with Apple, including requirements around account deletion and data handling. Counsel should confirm that this Policy’s account deletion and data handling provisions fully satisfy Apple’s requirements, including the requirement to offer in-app account deletion before App Store launch.

Item 5 — In-App Account Deletion Requirement (Apple)

Apple requires apps that support account creation to offer in-app account deletion before App Store approval (App Store Review Guideline 5.1.1(v)). The current deletion path is email-only (privacy@eastheightslabs.com). This is acceptable for TestFlight alpha, but must be remediated before App Store submission.
